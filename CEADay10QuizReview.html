Additional Architectural Styles

Key identifiers and descriptions for additional house styles mentioned throughout the quiz options:

Bungalow
Low-pitched roofs, wide overhanging eaves, prominent front porches, usually 1 to 1.5 stories.
Cape Cod
Simple 1 to 1.5-story frame building, steep gable roof, central chimney, unadorned exterior.
Cubic / Modern
Flat roofs, smooth geometric shapes, large glass windows, complete lack of decorative ornamentation.
Farmhouse
Functional design, simple rectangular footprint, prominent wrap-around or covered front porch, gable roof.
Greek Revival / Southern Colonial
Large multi-story entry porticos, grand classical columns (Doric, Ionic, Corinthian), symmetrical facade.
Neolithic
Primitive stone or thatch structures, turf or reed roofs, thick load-bearing wall construction.
Normandy (French Normandy)
Steeply pitched roofs, round stone or brick turrets with conical roofs, rustic masonry facades.
Prairie Style
Horizontal lines, low-pitched hipped roofs, deep overhangs, central chimneys, integrated landscape design.
Queen Anne
Complex multi-gable rooflines, round or octagonal corner towers, expansive wraparound porches, varied wall textures.
Saltbox
Asymmetrical roofline with a long, steeply sloped rear roof reaching to the first floor, central chimney, clapboard siding.
Shed Style
Multiple roof planes sloping in different directions, wood diagonal or vertical siding, asymmetrical window placements.
Tudor
Exposed decorative half-timbering against stucco or brick, tall narrow multi-pane windows, steep front gables.
